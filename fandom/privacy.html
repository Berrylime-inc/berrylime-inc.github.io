Fandom Roleplay — Privacy Policy

Last updated 23 September 2026 · Berrylime Labs LLC

Fandom Roleplay is made by Berrylime Labs. This policy is longer than we would like, because the app genuinely does send your writing somewhere, and you should know exactly where.

Your account

You sign in with an email address and a password, or with a link from a friend. We keep your email address, the names you chose, and a scrambled form of your password that cannot be turned back into it.

It is for adults

Signing up asks for your date of birth and refuses anyone under eighteen. We do not keep the date — only the fact that you confirmed you are an adult, and when.

What stays on your phone

Anything you have typed but not sent, your sign-in token, and a few small settings. Unsent writing is deliberately kept off our server.

What we keep on our server

Your books, in full: every message, every character, everything in the Lorebook, keepsakes, letters and scene summaries. Also the portraits you painted, your settings, your email address and your names. It sits on a server we run, hosted by Fly.io in the United States.

The app itself only talks to us

The only place Fandom Roleplay sends anything from your phone is our own server. Not an analytics company, not an advertiser, not a font service — nothing else, ever. Everything below happens on our side, after your writing reaches us.

Where your writing goes, and why

Every reply is written by a model that is not ours. To write a character's next line, our server sends Anthropic the book's title, its setting and world, the cast, the part of the Lorebook that character would know, and the recent lines of the scene. That means your fiction goes to Anthropic, in the United States, every turn. It is how the app works, and it is the most important sentence on this page. What Anthropic keeps is governed by their own terms, not ours.

Portraits. When you paint a character, our server sends a description to fal.ai. We strike out your cast's names, the fandom and the book's title first — but any other name you have typed into a character's identity goes as written. fal.ai never learns whose account it is. The picture comes back to us and is stored with your book.

Letters. If letters are switched on, sending one gives Resend your email address, the book's title, the character's name and the letter's first sentence, so it can deliver the mail. You can turn letters off on a book's back cover.

Sharing a book is making a copy

Sharing from the back cover copies the book, and its portraits, into a shelf every signed-in reader can open. Unsharing removes your copy — it cannot take back copies other people have already opened. Share accordingly.

Reporting a reply

Flagging a reply sends that reply's full text, the book and scene it came from, and the reason you gave, to a folder only we can read. We use it to fix what the characters say.

What we do not do

No analytics. No crash reporting. No advertising. No tracking of any kind, here or across other apps and websites. The app carries no third-party code beyond the framework it is built on. It never asks for your camera, microphone, photos, location or contacts, because it does not use them.

Deleting everything

"Delete my account and every book" removes your folder, your books, your portraits, your sign-in and your account record from our server. What survives is a monthly total of what the model cost us, which carries your tester name and no writing. We do not currently delete old books on a schedule — they stay until you delete them.
