Mochi — Privacy Policy

Last updated 22 September 2026 · Berrylime Labs

Mochi is made by Berrylime Labs. This policy is short because Mochi keeps almost nothing.

What Mochi reads

With your permission, Mochi reads and edits the reminders and calendar events already on your iPhone, through Apple's Reminders and Calendar frameworks. They stay in Apple's apps; Mochi keeps no second copy of them, and they are never uploaded.

What Mochi stores

On your phone only: your name (the one you typed for her), your settings, and a small snapshot for the widgets — the titles and due dates of the next few things — inside the app's private container. There is no account and no sign-in.

What leaves your phone

Three things, and nothing else.

What you say to her. The words you type or speak to Mochi are sent to a server run by Berrylime Labs, which passes them to Anthropic's model so they can be understood. Nothing else about you is sent with them. Our own server keeps no copy of it. It records a monthly total of what the model cost us, and the pass described below. No text, no images, no addresses, nothing that identifies you or your phone. The company that hosts that server, Fly.io, keeps its own connection logs, which include IP addresses, under their retention policy. Anthropic reads the text under their terms.

Proving the app is genuine. The first time Mochi uses our server, she asks your iPhone for an App Attest key — Apple's way of vouching that an app is a real, unmodified copy of itself on a real device — and sends that voucher to our server, which hands her back a pass. Apple built this so it says nothing about you or your phone beyond "this is a genuine copy of this app". It is not an advertising identifier, and it cannot be used to recognise you in any other app. Our server keeps that pass's key, the public key Apple vouched for, when it was issued, a count of how often it has been used, and whether it has been switched off — no text, no images, no addresses, nothing that identifies you or your phone. After about a month the pass expires and your phone makes a brand new one, so nothing from one month carries into the next, and deleting the app ends it.

If your iPhone can't do that, and some can't, Mochi does not use our server at all. She reads what you type on the phone itself — with Apple's on-device model where your phone has one, and with her own plain reading otherwise — and nothing leaves your phone.

Weather. Mochi asks Open-Meteo for the forecast using the location you chose. While you are choosing that location, the place name you type is sent to Open-Meteo so it can find it.

A screenshot you import. If you hand Mochi a screenshot of a schedule, it goes the same way — to our server and on to Anthropic's model to be read. Our server keeps no copy of it.

Speech

If you use the microphone, Apple's speech recogniser turns it into text. Where your device and language support it, that happens on your phone. Where they do not, Apple handles it on their servers. Either way, Mochi only ever receives the text.

The widgets

Mochi's widgets make no network requests at all. They show what is already on your phone.

Analytics and tracking

None. No analytics, no advertising identifier, and nothing that tracks you across other apps or websites. Mochi carries no third-party code at all — the app is built on Apple's own frameworks and nothing else.

Children

Mochi is rated 4+ and collects no personal data from anyone.

Erasing everything

Delete the app. Reminders and events you created through Mochi remain in Apple's Reminders and Calendar, because they were always yours; delete them there if you wish.
